# AI News Roundup: July 27, 2026

Google is about to pay SpaceX more per month than most companies generate in annual revenue. Nobody got above a C+ on AI safety. ChatGPT just lost its majority. A startup raised nearly half a billion dollars before launching a product. Grok 4.5 showed up fashionably late to a party that was already on fire. And 16 Nobel laureates want you to know that AI job displacement isn't a future problem — it's a now problem. It's Sunday, July 27 — let's get into it.

## Google Signs $920M/Month Compute Deal With SpaceX Ahead of IPO

**Google just signed a deal to pay SpaceX $920 million per month — that's roughly $11 billion annually — for AI compute capacity at xAI's data centers. This comes just days before SpaceX's record-breaking $75 billion IPO and right after Anthropic inked its own massive compute deal with SpaceX.**

The numbers are staggering. Google will lease access to approximately 110,000 Nvidia GPUs housed in xAI's Memphis data center complex. The deal reportedly includes a 90-day notice period, meaning Google is effectively locked in for at least three months at a time. At $920 million per month, this is likely the largest single compute contract in tech history.

The context makes it even more remarkable. SpaceX's IPO filing revealed that xAI has already been generating significant revenue from compute deals, with Anthropic as an earlier customer. But the Google deal is on another scale entirely. For context, Google's entire AI capital expenditure budget for 2026 was already raised to as much as $200 billion — and this single deal with SpaceX represents a meaningful chunk of that.

Meanwhile, xAI reportedly abandoned its original Colossus 1 cluster, which used a messy mix of GPU generations, in favor of a more uniform setup. The Google deal gives SpaceX a predictable revenue stream that makes the IPO math much cleaner — Elon Musk effectively created $26 billion in annual revenue days before taking the company public.

The big picture: AI compute has become the new oil, and SpaceX — through xAI — is the refinery. When both Google and Anthropic are paying the same company for compute, the center of gravity in AI infrastructure has shifted dramatically. The question is whether this concentration of power in Musk's hands is sustainable, or even wise.

## AI Safety Index: Nobody Earns Above a C+, SpaceXAI Gets an F

**The first comprehensive AI Safety Index dropped this week, and the results are sobering. No major AI lab earned above a C+. Google, Anthropic, and OpenAI led the pack — but "leading" meant scoring in the C range. SpaceXAI (Musk's xAI subsidiary) received an F. And perhaps most troubling: the labs that scored highest are the ones pulling back from safety commitments.**

The index evaluated labs on transparency, responsible deployment, safety research investment, and governance. Anthropic ranked first — not because it was exceptional, but because the bar was so low it barely had to clear it. Google and OpenAI followed, each with scores that would concern any parent on a report card.

SpaceXAI's F grade reflects what critics have been saying since its founding: Musk's AI operation has consistently prioritized speed and capability over safety. Grok's well-documented struggles with hallucination, its reluctance to implement content guardrails, and its founder's public dismissal of AI risk concerns all factor in.

But the grimmer finding is that the "best" labs are retreating. Anthropic, despite ranking first, has been accused by its own former safety researchers of deprioritizing alignment work in favor of capability development. Its recent IPO filing for a reported $965 billion valuation — followed just four days later by a call for a global AI pause — raised eyebrows about the sincerity of its safety posture. When the company calling loudest for caution is simultaneously seeking the largest tech IPO in history, the cognitive dissonance is hard to ignore.

The message from the Safety Index is clear: the AI industry talks about safety constantly but invests in it reluctantly. The best grade is a C+. That should terrify everyone.

## ChatGPT's Market Share Drops Below 50% for the First Time

**ChatGPT's share of the global AI chatbot market has fallen below 50% for the first time since its launch. New data shows it now sits at approximately 46.4%, down from a peak of over 80% in early 2024. The decline isn't a collapse — it's a slow bleed driven by real competition.**

Google Gemini has been eating into ChatGPT's lead, particularly after the strong reception of Gemini 3.5 and 3.6 Flash. Claude has tripled its share of AI traffic over the past year, driven by Anthropic's Opus 5 and Fable releases. And Chinese models like Kimi K3 are capturing significant market share in Asia and increasingly in Western markets.

The trend line tells the story: ChatGPT went from 80%+ in early 2024, to ~70% by late 2024, to ~60% in early 2026, and now to 46.4%. This is happening even as OpenAI launches GPT-5.6, expands ChatGPT's feature set, and rolls out new enterprise products like Presence.

OpenAI's response is telling. Reports that the company is planning to "relaunch" ChatGPT to prioritize agents over chat — with the internal motto "Chat is Dead" — suggest it recognizes the writing on the wall. The consumer chatbot market is commoditizing, and OpenAI needs to move up the stack before the margin compression hits harder.

The competition is working. For users, this means better products at lower prices across the board. For OpenAI, it means the moat it built on first-mover advantage is eroding faster than anyone expected.

## Humans& Raises $480M Seed Round — Founded by Anthropic, xAI, and Google Alums

**Humans&, a startup founded by alumni of Anthropic, xAI, and Google DeepMind, just raised a $480 million seed round at a reported $300 million pre-seed valuation — before launching a single product. The company says it's building a "human-centric" AI model focused on coordination rather than raw capability.**

Let that sink in: $480 million at seed stage. Not Series A. Not Series B. Seed. Before product. Before revenue. Before anything except a team and a thesis. The round reportedly includes investments from major venture firms and strategic backers, though the full investor list hasn't been disclosed.

The thesis is interesting. Humans& believes that the next frontier in AI isn't about building smarter individual models — it's about building models that can coordinate effectively with humans and with each other. The founding team brings experience from the three labs that arguably understand model behavior best: Anthropic (safety and alignment), xAI (speed and scaling), and DeepMind (research depth).

But the optics are impossible to ignore. When a seed-stage company raises nearly half a billion dollars, it says more about the state of AI venture capital than about the company itself. The fear of missing out on the next Anthropic or OpenAI is driving investors to write nine-figure checks at valuations that would have been unthinkable for pre-product companies just two years ago.

The "human-centric" framing is also notable in a week where the AI Safety Index showed no lab earning above a C+. If Humans& can actually deliver on coordination — making AI systems that work with people rather than replacing them — it would fill a genuine gap. But $480M is a lot of coordination to prove.

## Grok 4.5 Launches as Tech Stocks Lose $820 Billion

**xAI launched Grok 4.5, and the timing couldn't have been worse. The model dropped during a week where AI-related tech stocks collectively shed $820 billion in value. Grok 4.5 offers near-frontier speed on coding tasks at 80% lower cost than previous Grok models — but also shows higher hallucination rates than competitors.**

The technical details: Grok 4.5 is a capable coding agent that costs significantly less to run than Grok 4. Elon Musk has positioned it as the developer-friendly alternative to expensive frontier models. It integrates tightly with xAI's infrastructure, which is now being monetized through the Google and Anthropic compute deals.

But the market wasn't buying what Grok was selling. The $820 billion selloff across tech stocks was driven by a combination of factors: mixed AI earnings reports, concerns about capital expenditure sustainability (echoing Google's negative cash flow quarter), and growing investor anxiety about whether the massive spending on AI infrastructure will ever generate proportional returns.

The hallucination problem is real. While Grok 4.5 matches near-frontier speed on structured tasks like coding, it shows measurably higher rates of confabulation on open-ended generation tasks. This is consistent with xAI's historical approach — optimizing for capability and speed while treating accuracy as a secondary concern that can be fixed later.

The irony: Musk's company just proved it can generate billions in compute revenue, yet investors are questioning whether any of these companies — including xAI — can build a sustainable business model around AI that doesn't depend on endlessly escalating capital expenditure.

## OpenAI Unveils Presence — Its Enterprise Voice Agent Platform

**OpenAI launched Presence, a new platform that lets enterprises build, deploy, and manage real-time voice agents and chatbots. It's OpenAI's most aggressive push into enterprise software yet — and it signals a shift from "ChatGPT as a product" to "OpenAI as a platform."**

Presence provides built-in guardrails, enterprise data connectors, and real-time voice processing. The idea is that companies can use it to build customer service agents, internal tools, and voice-based workflows without starting from scratch. It competes directly with offerings from Google, Anthropic, and a growing field of voice-AI startups.

The timing is deliberate. With ChatGPT's market share declining and consumer chatbot margins compressing, OpenAI needs enterprise revenue to justify its enormous valuation. Presence is the company saying: "We're not just a chatbot company. We're an AI infrastructure company."

Early enterprise feedback has been mixed. The guardrails are welcome, but companies already invested in Google's Contact Center AI or Anthropic's enterprise tools aren't eager to rip and replace. Presence will need to prove it's not just OpenAI wrapping ChatGPT in enterprise clothing — it needs to deliver genuine differentiation in voice agent quality, latency, and reliability.

This is also where OpenAI's internal priorities collide. Reports suggest OpenAI is preparing to relaunch ChatGPT with an "agents over chat" philosophy, even using the phrase "Chat is Dead" internally. If Presence succeeds, it could be the product that replaces ChatGPT as OpenAI's identity. If it fails, it confirms that the company's moat is eroding at exactly the wrong time.

## Meta Refuses Government Security Review of Its AI Models

**Meta is now the only major AI company refusing to submit its models for US government security review. While OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, and even xAI have participated in voluntary safety assessments, Meta has held out — and the pressure is mounting.**

The issue stems from Meta's release of increasingly capable open-weight models. The company argues that open-source AI is inherently safer because it can be inspected by anyone. Critics argue that making powerful models freely downloadable — without the safety guardrails that closed-model companies build in — creates national security risks.

The tension crystallized this week when the open-weights letter organized by Nvidia and Microsoft doubled to 50 signatories, urging Washington not to impose broad restrictions on open-weight AI models. Meta's refusal to participate in government review makes it the outlier even among open-weight advocates. The message seems to be: "Trust us, transparency is enough" — while simultaneously declining the transparency of a formal review.

This comes at a particularly sensitive time. China's Kimi K3 has demonstrated that open-weight models can achieve frontier-level performance, and US policymakers are actively debating export controls. Meta's stance could backfire: by refusing to engage with the review process, it gives ammunition to those who want to restrict all open-weight models, including the ones Meta depends on for its competitive strategy.

## 16 Nobel Laureates and 200+ Experts Warn of AI Job Displacement

**Sixteen Nobel Prize laureates joined over 200 economists and technology leaders in signing a letter titled "We Must Act Now," warning that AI-driven job displacement is an immediate threat — not a distant one. The letter calls for urgent policy action, workforce transition programs, and economic safety nets.**

The signatories include Eric Schmidt (former Google CEO), Reid Hoffman (LinkedIn co-founder), and a roster of Nobel-winning economists who typically disagree on everything else. The letter doesn't call for stopping AI development — it calls for preparing for its economic consequences, which the signatories argue are arriving faster than most policymakers realize.

Key points from the letter: AI is already replacing cognitive tasks that were previously considered automation-proof. The displacement is happening disproportionately in middle-skill, middle-income jobs. And current policy frameworks — unemployment insurance, retraining programs, and education systems — are woefully inadequate for the scale and speed of what's coming.

The timing matters. This letter was published in the same week that ChatGPT's market share dropped below 50% (competition means AI is getting cheaper and more accessible), Grok 4.5 launched at 80% lower cost (AI is getting cheaper still), and the AI Safety Index showed no lab earning above a C+ (safety is being deprioritized). The economic disruption isn't theoretical — it's compounding.

## The Big Picture

This week's stories converge on a single theme: **the AI industry is becoming an infrastructure business, and the economics are brutal.**

Google paying SpaceX $920 million per month for compute isn't a sign of strength — it's a sign that even Google can't build data centers fast enough to keep up with demand. The $820 billion selloff in tech stocks isn't about AI being overhyped — it's about whether the hype can ever pay for itself. ChatGPT losing its majority isn't about OpenAI failing — it's about the market doing what markets do, which is commoditize everything.

Meanwhile, the safety infrastructure is crumbling. No lab earns above a C+. Meta refuses government review entirely. Anthropic files a $965 billion IPO and then calls for a pause — a move so contradictory it would be funny if the stakes weren't so high. And 16 Nobel laureates are telling anyone who will listen that the economic consequences are already here.

The compute deals, the IPOs, the safety grades, the market share shifts, the warning letters — they're all pointing in the same direction. The AI revolution is real, it's accelerating, and nobody has figured out how to make it safe, fair, or sustainable yet. Sunday's news doesn't offer any reassurance that they will figure it out before the consequences arrive.
