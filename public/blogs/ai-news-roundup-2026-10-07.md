The theme of today's AI news is simple: **agents are moving from demos into the org chart**. OpenAI spent October 6 signing distribution deals for its GPT-6 agent stack, publishing fresh evidence that its agents can handle genuinely complex professional work, and — per Bloomberg's continuing coverage — lining up as much as $30 billion in new capital at a valuation approaching $1.4 trillion, all while two of its biggest enterprise customers quietly dial back their dependence on Anthropic's Claude. Here's what matters, in plain terms.

## OpenAI expands its Atlassian partnership: GPT-6 agents come to Jira and Rovo

OpenAI and Atlassian announced an expanded multi-year partnership on October 6 that puts OpenAI's GPT-6 family of models — including frontier model **GPT-6 Astra** — deeper into Atlassian's platform, powering AI agents across the collaboration stack and its Rovo agent product line.

The headline experiment: Atlassian is exploring **new Jira integrations that let teams assign project work directly to AI agents**, effectively making an "agent" another assignee in the board alongside human engineers. Underpinning this is work on **Atlassian MCP (Model Context Protocol) Server connectors**, which would link OpenAI's agents to Atlassian's data layer — Jira issues, Confluence docs, service management context — so agents can act with real organizational context instead of working from screenshots.

There's also a measurement angle worth watching. Atlassian and OpenAI are partnering with **DX**, the developer-productivity platform, to help engineering teams actually quantify what AI coding agents contribute — the metrics question every CTO will face once agents start committing code at scale. This deepens a collaboration going back to 2023, when Atlassian built Rovo on OpenAI models.

## OpenAI × Ironclad: agents score 55% on complex legal workflows — up from 41.6%

Alongside the Atlassian deal, OpenAI published new computer-use research with **Ironclad**, the contract lifecycle management platform, and the numbers show a step-change on genuinely hard professional work — not toy benchmarks.

On **11 complex legal and contracting workflows** (the kind that take a human professional **30–40 minutes each**), OpenAI's evaluations showed:

- **GPT-6 Astra averaged 55.0%** across the full dataset
- The previous generation **GPT-5.6 Sol averaged 41.6%**
- An internal development model reached **63.7%**

Ironclad is notable because it's the **first software company OpenAI has partnered with to train and evaluate agents on complex professional work** at this level — using Ironclad's domain (digital contracting) as a testbed for how AI handles multi-step, high-stakes, contract-heavy tasks. The results were published under OpenAI's "Advancing computer use with Ironclad" write-up.

Two things to take from this: first, model deltas on *hard* workflows are still large — a 13+ point jump between generations on tasks this complex is meaningful. Second, the evaluation methodology (real workflows, real software, long-horizon tasks) is becoming the standard way labs prove agent claims, and professional-software companies are emerging as the benchmark partners of choice.

## Meta and Microsoft are quietly weaning engineers off Claude

A new report from The Information, picked up widely on October 6, details how **Meta and Microsoft are actively reducing their internal reliance on Anthropic's Claude** — and the specifics are striking:

- **Microsoft** initially projected spending **over $1 billion this year** on Anthropic technology, but has cut its projected internal spend by **more than a third**. Executives Scott Guthrie and Jay Parikh have steered employees toward in-house options and OpenAI models via **GitHub Copilot**. In the cloud division, the monthly per-employee AI budget reportedly dropped from about **$100,000 to roughly $10,000**. Notably, *customer* spending on Anthropic models through Microsoft's enterprise platforms keeps growing — it's Microsoft's own house that's switching.
- **Meta** saw internal usage of **Claude Code fall from roughly 60,000 employees earlier this year to about 30,000** recently. Spring layoffs (about 10% of its 78,000-person workforce) explain part of the drop, but the bigger driver is Meta pushing its own tools — **MetaCode (30,000+ internal users)** and **Muse Code (6,000+)**. For scale: Meta reportedly spent **over $105 million on Claude Code in a single 28-day stretch**.

The strategic read: Anthropic has transformed from a useful supplier into a direct competitor (its agent-facing products now compete for the same "AI teammate" workloads), so Meta and Microsoft see little reason to keep funding their rival while building their own stacks. Reports also suggest Meta has sought to restrict Anthropic's access to its training data. Expect more "internal-first" AI procurement policies across big tech over the next few quarters.

## CrowdStrike, AWS and Nvidia open applications for their agentic-security startup accelerator

CrowdStrike announced on October 6 the **fourth annual Cybersecurity Startup Accelerator**, run with **Amazon Web Services and Nvidia**, focused on — what else — **the agentic era** of security: startups building defenses (and attack surfaces) for environments full of autonomous AI agents.

Key details:

- **Applications are open worldwide through November 2, 2026**; the eight-week program runs **January 11 – March 8, 2027**
- Up to **ten finalists pitch live at RSAC 2027** (April 5–8), where a winner is selected
- AWS contributes Partner Network onboarding, technical validation and Marketplace listings; Nvidia offers training and developer resources via its **Inception** program
- Outstanding companies may be considered for investment from the **CrowdStrike Falcon Fund**

The program has supported more than 90 startups across its first three cohorts. If you're building agentic security tooling, this is one of the highest-leverage, equity-free programs in the category.

## Still trending: OpenAI's reported $30B round at a ~$1.4T valuation

First reported Monday by Bloomberg and still developing into Tuesday: **OpenAI is in talks with UAE investment funds — anchored by Abu Dhabi's MGX — and BlackRock to raise at least $30 billion** at a valuation of roughly **$1.4 trillion**.

What's known so far:

- The UAE funds have discussed contributing **up to $10 billion collectively** as a syndicate; MGX — chaired by Sheikh Tahnoon bin Zayed Al Nahyan — already holds stakes in OpenAI, Anthropic and xAI, and has raised nearly $50 billion this year for AI investments
- **BlackRock is in talks to participate**, while existing backers **Thrive Capital and Andreessen Horowitz** have reportedly considered joining
- No lead investor has been chosen, and OpenAI is said to be offering the round at a **self-set price** rather than negotiating terms
- The raise comes as OpenAI **delays its IPO until at least next year**; its last round was $122 billion in March at an $852 billion valuation

Both OpenAI and BlackRock declined to comment, and MGX didn't respond to inquiries — terms could still change. But if the size and valuation hold, this would be one of the largest private raises ever recorded.
