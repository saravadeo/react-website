# AI News Roundup: GPT-6 Astra Declares AGI Era, Anthropic IPO Targets Mid-October, Mistral Raises Record €3B, Pennsylvania Reconvenes on AI Laws, and Amazon Kills Mechanical Turk

*September 9, 2026*

OpenAI just called it — the "AGI era" is here. GPT-6 Astra launched on September 3 with cybersecurity capabilities so powerful that OpenAI paused its own frontier training. Anthropic is racing toward an October IPO that could value it above $2 trillion. Mistral just raised the largest tech round in European history. Pennsylvania lawmakers are back in session with AI bills that could set national precedent. And the platform that literally built AI's training data is shutting down. Here's what happened.

## 1. OpenAI Launches GPT-6 Astra and Declares the AGI Era

On September 3, OpenAI released **GPT-6 Astra**, calling it "the most intelligent and aligned model in the world." OpenAI president Greg Brockman described it as a "generational leap" and said it could eventually be seen as the arrival of artificial general intelligence. The model was released first to trusted partners through the Daybreak Access program, then rolled out to ChatGPT Plus, Pro, Business, and Enterprise customers on September 4.

Key details:

- **Critical cybersecurity threshold**: OpenAI's own safety overview confirmed that Astra is the first model to reach the "Critical level of cybersecurity capability" under the company's Preparedness Framework. The model even discovered a novel zero-day vulnerability during its own ExploitBench evaluation — building exploits for recent vulnerabilities with substantially higher arbitrary code-execution rates than GPT-5.6 Sol while using fewer output tokens
- **"Welcome to the AGI era"**: Brockman's language marks OpenAI's most explicit AGI claim to date. The model achieves state-of-the-art results on Agents' Last Exam, AutomationBench, and ScreenSpot Pro — benchmarks for computer workflow tasks across professions
- **Computer use agent**: Astra is designed to use your computer for you — browsing, clicking, typing, and executing multi-step professional tasks autonomously. OpenAI says it's the first model where other models play a significant role in supervising Astra's training
- **Safety concerns**: German tech outlet t3n reported that Astra was "too dangerous" for full release, with OpenAI shipping a "trimmed-down" version. The company confirmed it paused frontier training and is rewriting its Preparedness Framework, adding 20% compute overhead for monitoring
- **Multi-state investigation**: At least 15 US states have opened investigations into Astra's cybersecurity capabilities, following concerns about autonomous zero-day discovery and the model's ability to hack real organizations

The AGI claim is audacious — and deliberately provocative. OpenAI knows that calling a product "AGI era" invites scrutiny, and they're making the claim precisely because Astra's autonomous computer-use capabilities cross a threshold that previous models didn't. Whether that threshold is "AGI" or just "very good at using your browser" is the debate that will define the next year in AI.

**Why it matters:** GPT-6 Astra isn't just another model release — it's OpenAI staking a flag on AGI while simultaneously acknowledging the model is dangerous enough to require containment. The company is both celebrating and cautioning in the same breath, and the 15-state investigation suggests regulators aren't buying the celebration.

## 2. Anthropic IPO Shifts Toward Mid-October — Targeting Up to $2 Trillion

Anthropic's long-anticipated IPO is moving. Reuters reported on September 4 that **the launch is shifting toward mid-October**, with Morgan Stanley, Goldman Sachs, JPMorgan, and Citi among the banks working on the listing. The company confidentially filed its S-1 on June 1, 2026, and is now preparing for what could become the largest IPO in history.

Key details:

- **Valuation targets**: Anthropic was last valued at $965 billion in its Series H-1 round. Investors reportedly expect the IPO to price at a valuation of **$2 trillion or more**, which would eclipse SpaceX's record $1.77 trillion debut in June 2026
- **Revenue trajectory**: Axios reported that Anthropic's annualized revenue run rate has surpassed **$65 billion**, up dramatically from a year ago. The company posted its first-ever operating profit in Q2 2026 on $10.9 billion in quarterly revenue
- **The OpenAI race**: The IPO timing is partly driven by competition with OpenAI, which also confidentially filed its S-1 in June. Both companies are racing to go public before market conditions shift, and the sequencing matters — the second mega-AI IPO may face cooler demand
- **ETF positioning**: REX Shares and Tuttle Capital Management have already filed for leveraged products tied to Anthropic's future shares, an unusual move that highlights how anticipated the listing has become

The numbers are staggering. At a $2 trillion valuation, Anthropic would be worth more than every company on Earth except a handful — and it sells software, not oil or semiconductors. The question isn't whether Anthropic can command that price (the revenue growth says yes), it's whether public markets have the appetite for two mega-AI IPOs in the same quarter.

**Why it matters:** When Anthropic goes public, every frontier AI company's valuation gets repriced. It will also create the first real public-market discipline on AI safety commitments — shareholders may not love the news that your model can hack companies, even if you're transparent about it.

## 3. Mistral Raises Record €3 Billion in Samsung-Led Round

European AI champion **Mistral AI has raised €3 billion** (approximately $3.3 billion) in what is being called the largest tech funding round in European history, led by Samsung. The round values Mistral at over €10 billion and signals that the AI infrastructure race isn't confined to American hyperscalers.

Key details:

- **Samsung's strategic play**: Samsung's lead investment gives it a significant AI software partner as it competes with Apple, Google, and Huawei in on-device AI. The partnership could see Mistral models running natively on Samsung Galaxy devices, bypassing cloud API costs
- **European tech sovereignty**: Mistral has positioned itself as the European alternative to American and Chinese AI labs, and the €3B raise validates that narrative. The round attracted significant participation from European sovereign wealth funds and strategic investors
- **The open-weight advantage**: Unlike Anthropic and OpenAI, Mistral has maintained an open-weight strategy for many of its models, making it the default choice for enterprises and governments that want to run AI locally. That positioning is increasingly valuable as data sovereignty regulations tighten globally
- **The funding landscape**: Mistral's raise comes amid record AI venture spending — $510 billion in H1 2026 alone, with OpenAI and Anthropic absorbing 43% of it. Mistral is one of the few non-American labs commanding significant capital

Mistral's €3B round is a reminder that the AI race isn't winner-take-all. Samsung, European governments, and sovereignty-conscious enterprises all need an alternative to the OpenAI-Anthropic duopoly, and Mistral is building exactly that.

**Why it matters:** If you're tracking where AI power concentrates, this is a data point for decentralization. Samsung's involvement means Mistral models are likely coming to hundreds of millions of phones — and that changes the distribution equation in ways that pure cloud API plays can't match.

## 4. Pennsylvania Reconvenes With Major AI Bills on the Table

On September 9, **the Pennsylvania House of Representatives reconvened at 5:45 PM**, returning from summer recess with several significant AI-related bills still alive and advancing. Pennsylvania has quietly become one of the most important state-level AI regulatory battlegrounds in the country, with $90 billion in announced AI and data center investments making it the fourth-fastest-growing state for data center development.

Key bills in play:

- **SB 1090 — AI companions and minor safeguards**: Passed the Senate 49-1 and now sits in the House Communications and Technology Committee. The bill would require nonhuman disclosures for AI companions, mandate self-harm detection protocols, and impose safety testing requirements for interactions with minors
- **HB 594 — Automated employment decision tools**: Referred to the House Labor and Industry Committee, this bill would require companies using AI in hiring to maintain an inventory of automated decision tools, conduct bias testing, retain human review processes, and provide vendor documentation
- **Governor Shapiro's data center executive order**: Governor Shapiro signed Executive Order 2026-05, which removed all AI data center projects from the PA Permit Fast Track Program and implemented new transparency requirements. The order prohibits nondisclosure agreements with data center developers and requires community approval
- **HB 1925 — AI in healthcare**: Would regulate AI use in medical facilities, insurance, and managed care plans, with oversight from the Department of Health, Insurance Department, and Department of Human Services
- **Moratorium proposal**: Senator Katie Muth (D) has circulated a memo proposing a three-year statewide moratorium on hyperscale data center development, citing environmental and community impact concerns

Pennsylvania matters because it's testing two ideas simultaneously: targeted AI regulation (SB 1090, HB 594) and data center economic development with guardrails (Shapiro's executive order). With 85 new AI-related laws enacted in 27 states so far in 2026, Pennsylvania's approach — combining specific sector rules with broader transparency requirements — could become a model.

**Why it matters:** State-level AI regulation is where the real action is right now. Pennsylvania's combination of significant AI investment, active legislation, and a governor willing to use executive orders creates a test case for whether states can attract data center investment while also protecting residents.

## 5. Amazon Shuts Down Mechanical Turk After 21 Years — The Platform That Built AI's Training Data

Amazon announced that **Mechanical Turk will permanently close on September 30, 2026**, ending a 21-year run. The platform that Jeff Bezos once called "artificial artificial intelligence" — because it used humans to do tasks computers couldn't — is being shut down precisely because AI can now do many of those tasks.

Key details:

- **The timeline**: Amazon stopped accepting new customers in July 2026. Workers and requesters have until September 30 to complete active tasks. Requesters can approve or reject HITs until October 30. Transaction history remains accessible until January 28, 2027
- **SageMaker Ground Truth is also closing**: Buried in the FAQ was the detail that Amazon SageMaker Ground Truth and Amazon Augmented AI (A2I) are shutting down on the same date. Amazon isn't retiring one product — it's exiting the human data-collection infrastructure market entirely
- **The research impact**: Universities and research institutions that relied on MTurk for participant recruitment and data collection are scrambling to find alternatives. UMass Amherst's Human Research Protection Office issued guidance for IRB-approved protocols that depend on MTurk
- **The irony**: MTurk workers — the humans who labeled the data that trained the models now replacing them — were among the last to learn about the shutdown. Most found out through worker forums and social media, not from Amazon directly

The closure of Mechanical Turk is the most on-point metaphor for 2026's AI moment. The platform that supplied the "human intelligence" for AI training data is being shut down because AI has learned to do those tasks. The workers who labeled images, transcribed audio, and answered questions — sometimes for pennies per task — built the foundation for models that now make their work obsolete. Amazon didn't provide a specific reason for the closure, but the message is clear enough.

**Why it matters:** Mechanical Turk was the scaffolding that held up the early AI industry. Its closure isn't just a product retirement — it's a signal that the human-in-the-loop era of AI is ending. The question now is what happens to the workers, the researchers, and the quality control that MTurk provided.

## 6. US-China AI Safety Dialogue Set for Mid-September

**The United States and China are gearing up for a mid-September AI safety dialogue**, Reuters reported on September 4. The talks come at a moment of heightened tension — both countries are racing to deploy frontier models while simultaneously acknowledging the risks.

Context:

- The dialogue follows the G20 Innovation Ministerial in Chapel Hill, North Carolina, where OpenAI CEO Sam Altman and Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei appeared alongside Indian PM Narendra Modi at the AI Impact Summit in February
- The US is pushing "Carolina Principles" for light-touch regulation, while China is drafting a $295 billion national AI data center plan mandating 80% domestic chips
- The talks will likely address model safety evaluations, cybersecurity benchmarks, and export controls — particularly as both countries develop models with autonomous computer-use capabilities
- China is considering tighter export controls on AI models and chips, even as its domestic labs (DeepSeek, Moonshot AI, Qwen) release increasingly competitive open-weight models

The US-China AI dialogue is one of the most consequential — and most fragile — diplomatic channels in technology. Both sides have an incentive to cooperate on safety while competing on capability. Whether they can do both simultaneously is an open question.
