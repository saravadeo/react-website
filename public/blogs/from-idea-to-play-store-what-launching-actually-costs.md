# From Idea to Play Store: What Launching Actually Costs

On July 15, I opened a blank document and wrote one number at the top: **1,000 Google Play downloads by July 31.**

Sixteen days later the campaign window closed. The real count — as measured as anyone could measure it — was **fewer than ten**.

Getting an app from idea to the Play Store is the famous part of the story. Build, test, ship, celebrate. What nobody warns you about is the cost that arrives *after* day one. My budget for the whole push was ₹0, so this isn't a story about money. It's the receipt for what launching actually costs, itemized the hard way.

## A Plan Priced in Optimism

ChallengeCam had just shipped — a challenge video maker that records your face cam and the challenge overlay in one take, six categories, export straight to Reels and Shorts. It was live on the Play Store at roughly zero installs, which meant the only thing standing between it and 1,000 users was distribution.

So I built a marketing plan the way engineers build systems: tiered, estimated, complete.

- **Reddit:** 150–300 downloads, across seven subreddits
- **Product Hunt:** 100–200
- **Instagram Reels:** 100–200
- **YouTube Shorts:** 50–100
- **TikTok:** 50–150
- Then a lower tier — Quora, Indie Hackers, app directories, SEO blog posts — to mop up the rest

Add it up and the plan promised **400–900 installs** from free channels in sixteen days. And I did the homework, genuinely. On day zero I had five Reddit post drafts, each angled for a different subreddit, a Product Hunt launch draft, two Twitter threads, three Quora answers, SEO blog drafts, and a tracker with checkboxes for every day of the campaign.

The plan was complete. That was exactly its problem. Complete *on paper*.

## What the Plan Didn't Price In

If you read the tracker day by day, one line starts repeating itself, slightly rephrased, for sixteen days straight:

> **BLOCKED: needs Onkar's Reddit account.**

That's me, blocked by my own tracker.

The estimates assumed posts. The posts assumed **me** — my Reddit account, my karma history, my Product Hunt login, my name on Quora answers. Every high-impact channel had a human gate, and the human was the busiest component in the system. My days already ran 10 AM to 9 PM. The plan even knew that — the evening nudge was designed to land at 10 PM IST and ask for five-minute actions. But you can't schedule willpower you don't have left.

Sixteen days of checkboxes. Exactly one cleared: a single post in r/IMadeThis on Day 6.

Even the scoreboard had a gate. Play Store renders install counts with JavaScript, and I had no console access, so during the entire campaign the tracker logged *estimates* — "still <10." The one number everyone was optimizing for was a guess.

July 31 arrived. Campaign closed. **Fewer than 10 downloads**, against a plan that promised 400–900.

## The One Channel That Never Needed Me

While I wasn't posting, a machine was.

The website's blog — six SEO posts at launch — had grown into a daily pipeline that publishes one post every day without touching my accounts. From August 1 it shipped daily: **93 posts by late September, 113 pages live now, a 69-day streak** as of today.

And the download badge crawled upward — in Google's only public measurement for small apps, a text badge buried in the page HTML, which I now grep with a browser user-agent like it's a log file:

- **5+** — early August
- **10+** — late September
- **50+** — early October, holding since

Slow. Comically slow next to the plan. But moving. And eighty-five days after launch, the blog is the only channel on the dashboard that never logged a BLOCKED day.

The irony deserves underlining: in the plan, the blog was **Tier 3 — long tail**, estimated at 20–50 downloads *total*. It has outdelivered every channel ranked above it, because it was the only one that shipped.

## What Launching Actually Costs

Here's the real receipt. Three line items.

**1. The gap between ready and live.** Twelve platform submissions are still sitting at "todo" — copy polished since September 29, BetaList now 80+ days overdue on a checklist nobody signed. Nothing taught me this more precisely: **the distance between prepared and published is the most expensive distance in marketing.** Assets don't ship themselves, and a plan that assumes they will is fiction.

**2. The founder as a single point of failure.** In engineering, you design systems with no single points of failure. My launch plan had one — me. The most valuable channels were exactly the ones that required my hands, my voice, my accounts. I built a distribution system whose bottleneck was the person with the least spare capacity.

**3. Humility, nightly.** The daily check-in cron survived the dead goal. It's on day 85 as I write this — every evening at 10 PM IST it sends one scoreboard line and asks for one or two five-minute actions. Typing "50+, unchanged" night after night is a real cost. It's also the cheapest insurance I've ever bought, because the alternative is discovering in November that nothing has shipped since July.

## The Bigger Insight

Launch plans price channels by reach. Reality prices them by one question: **does it run when you're busy?**

"Marketing is a daily practice, not a launch-day event" is one of those phrases every founder nods at. My tracker made it land for real: the splash had a two-week window, and missed. The drip has run 69 straight days, and is still compounding.

So here's the rule I'm taking forward:

**Build the machine first, then rent the founder for the moments machines can't cover.**

The Reddit post only I can honestly write, the Product Hunt story that only carries if it's mine — those are founder moments. They get protected time. Everything else should ship without me.

There's no deadline on the next milestone. The badge says 50+, and 100+ is next. No countdown this time — the app has a machine now, and machines don't need deadlines. They need days.

And they're getting days.
